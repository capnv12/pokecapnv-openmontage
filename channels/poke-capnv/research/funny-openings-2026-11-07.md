# What makes opening Shorts funny, and which humor is unused on Pokemon channels (2026-11-07)

Scope. The earlier research covered structure (hooks, length, endings, money). This one covers only the joke: what is actually funny, and can a faceless, AI-voiced Pokemon channel do it.

Method in one paragraph. About 2,100 listings came back from YouTube hashtag pages and search pages (search by keyword alone returns almost no Shorts, so I used the "short videos only" search filter and hashtag pages). From roughly 500 usable candidates I downloaded 40 at 480p (the limit), made a contact sheet of frames every 2 seconds for each, and a faster-whisper transcript. I also pulled the latest 40 Shorts titles from 13 Pokemon channels plus 7 other channels and compared their top videos by title and view count. View counts and subscriber counts are as of 2026-11-07.

The most important finding first, in plain words: almost nothing that is genuinely funny on Shorts is a plain pack opening. Of the 40 videos I watched, only the Optional Rules channel (7 of the 40) has a joke that lives inside the opening itself. The rest are skits where a card is a prop, reaction edits, or compilations. That is a real gap in the market, not a gap in my search, but it also means the evidence for "funny opening, no face" rests mostly on one channel.

## (a) Verdict: the humor engines that fit a faceless AI-voice Pokemon channel and are unused there

### Engine 1. Hobby-seriousness transplant (the voice treats something absurd as a real collectible set)

What it is. The voice talks exactly like a card collector ("chasing the rare foil", "out of print", "the rest of the set is trash, abysmal launch") over footage that is not cards at all. The hands are real, the footage is real (a moth cocoon, a hornet stinger, a pitcher plant, a baby bird), and the made-up card names are the jokes ("Timmy Tapeworm", "Salmonella", "rare choking hazards, amber grids, sapphire suffocator", "lemon luke", "the big booger"). Nobody explains it is a joke.

Proof, all from channel Optional Rules (180,000 subscribers; its 40 latest Shorts have a middle view count of 89,000, so a typical video there gets about 89 thousand views, and 23 of the 40 are "booster pack" titles):
- "opening moth booster packs": 62.0 million views, 20 seconds. The voice opens a "steel cocoon pack", says "the rest of the set is trash, this has been an abysmal launch", then screams at the hit at about 10 seconds.
- "opening baby bird booster from the new respiratory rescue set": 44.2 million views, 18 seconds. Laugh beat at about 8.5 seconds ("really hoping for one of the rare choking hazards, amber grids, sapphire suffocator").
- "opening tubular leaf booster packs": 12.1 million views, 17 seconds. The payoff is a disappointment: "Out of all the packs, we got lemon luke... oh my god, no."
- "opening special collector's tree sack booster packs": 868 thousand views, 11 seconds ("look at all the striped sallies in there!").
- "opening howling hornet booster packs": 382 thousand views, 32 seconds ("kind of hoping to get Timmy Tapeworm or Salmonella", then a rant that it is "better to leave them sealed but the resell value isn't fantastic").
- Same channel, same joke, not an opening: "these leaf booster pack scalpers are getting out of control" (117 thousand views, 10 seconds, a voice complaining about scalpers of a set that does not exist).

Why it fits. No face (hands only, in several videos not even hands of a person doing a pack). The voice carries 100 percent of the joke, and the voice is a flat, sincere collector tone that an AI voice does well; in fact the sincerity is the joke. It survives a dud pack because the dud IS often the punchline ("we got lemon luke, no"; "the rest of the set is trash").

Is it used on Pokemon channels? No. None of the 13 channels' titles or top videos use a transplant or invented-set joke.

Honest limit. The proof is on non-card footage. The adaptation to real Pokemon cards is a design step I have not seen proven: the voice keeps the invented-hobby language (named junk tiers, fake market lore, a "set review" of a real set's commons, lovingly serious names for bulk cards) over real pulls. Risk: the 62M and 44M videos get their surprise from the footage not being cards; with real cards that surprise is gone and only the voice remains. Test it on 2 or 3 packs before committing.

### Engine 2. Cut-away consequence gag (the edit punishes the action)

What it is. A single cut to unrelated footage as the punchline. In "Don't rip and drive" (channel PokePill, 14,900 subscribers, 5.97 million views, 15 seconds), the video opens on road footage, then hands rip packs in a car, and the cut implies the consequence. Hands only, no face, no spoken words in the transcript.

Proof is one video only; treat as promising, not proven. The related "Norway vs England pack battle" (Football fortune teller, 23,100 subscribers, 1.74 million views, 36 seconds, black gloves) shows another hands-only editing trick (a live scoreboard that ticks up as cards are revealed) but that one is structure, not humor, so I do not count it as an engine.

Why it fits. It needs only an edit, no voice and no face. It works on any pack, since the joke is about the action, not the pull. The AI voice can narrate the setup with a straight face.

Used on Pokemon channels? No cut-away gags in the titles or top videos of the 13 channels. (UnlistedLeaf and RealBreakingNate use stunts, which are different: the stunt is the premise, not a one-cut punchline.)

### Engine 3. Deadpan literal Pokedex reading (the voice reads the real flavor text on the pulled card as if it were evidence)

What it is. The card's real Pokedex entry or attack text read flatly. Proof that the topic itself makes people laugh and watch, outside openings: "If Pokedex Entries were EVEN MORE Literal" (Dorkly, 3.96 million views, 127 seconds), "Cursed Pokedex Entry" (unzipped cartoons, 2.73 million, 17 seconds), "These Pokedex entries HAVE TO BE WRONG!" (VibeCheckup, 2.45 million, 89 seconds), "Memorizing Pokedex entries is diabolical" (LegendaryPokeman, 1.58 million, 69 seconds, which I watched: a face-on-camera guessing game, "it is controlled by mushroom" and the quiz partner deadpanning that it is Parasect), plus Jordblast type-by-type entry videos at 64 to 100 thousand views each.

Not proven on openings. I found zero pack-opening Shorts that do this, which is the reason it is unused: nobody has tried it. It is the single best match to your brief's own example ("reads the real Pokedex entry deadpan"). It needs no face (the entry is on the card or the screen), the AI voice is ideal for a flat literal read, and a dud pack is fine, since commons have Pokedex entries too (and the creepy ones are often on commons).

Used on Pokemon channels? Not by the 13 (Leonhart does lore, not entry reading).

### Engine 4. Wrong-place premise with a physical sting (pack found or opened somewhere absurd, something goes wrong to the hands)

What it is. "This Destined Rivals pack is covered in pond scum!" (ChaseCollective, 1,530 subscribers, 1.91 million views, 40 seconds): "Fishing for Pokemon", a sealed pack pulled from a pond, hands only, and the line "dude, the fish spiked me" at the end. A tiny channel with a big result means the premise carried it, not the audience size.

Why it fits. Hands and voice only. Dud packs do not matter because the interest is the setting. Weaker evidence: one video. Partly used on Pokemon channels (UnlistedLeaf "eating all McDonalds x Pokemon cards", PokeNibbles trick-or-treat packs, RealBreakingNate gold-frog hunts are stunt premises), so this one is "partly used", ranked fourth for that reason. Because you are bored with skit and costume framings, use it only as a one-off.

### Not recommended even though they scored big

- Face contrast edits (TM32, 5.2 and 6.0 million views): rely on a deadpan face next to a hyper celebrating self. Face required.
- Ranking compilations of reactions (DayNcool "Ranking the Best Glitter Dumpling Unboxing Moments", 100.8 million views): funny because of strangers' faces and screams. Face required.
- Fake-reaction parody (Shiny Vert, 4.77 million): mocks fake reactions with the creator's face. Face required.
- Kid/family skits (Aether254 12.5 million, PokeRetro 4.1 million): characters and acting; you rejected skits.

## (c) Engine clusters

For each engine: videos, whether a face is needed, whether it works with a voiceover, whether it survives a dud, and whether the 13 Pokemon channels use it (judged from their latest 40 Shorts titles and top 8 videos by views; I did not watch all of them).

1. Hobby-seriousness transplant (fake set talk)
   - Videos: 1vu7U9ERIs4, mVI9kIBOIoo, Liz7P1kQivE, nl4YTnNjbLE, 2Ow8auc1euU, FqvvvQQYlCw, Mu-vOhCJAQA (7 of the 40, all Optional Rules).
   - Face needed: no. Voiceover: required, it is the engine. Dud pack: survives, the dud is a punchline. Used by the 13 Pokemon channels: no.
   - Score: faceless OK, voice OK, dud OK, unused. Passes all four.
2. Cut-away consequence gag
   - Videos: niMXRlPINxY (one video).
   - Face: no. Voiceover: works (not tested). Dud: independent of pulls. Used by the 13: no.
   - Passes all four on one sample. Low certainty.
3. Deadpan literal Pokedex reading
   - Videos watched: SsJXeMHH-CY (with a face). Non-watched view evidence: Dorkly, unzipped cartoons, VibeCheckup, Jordblast (titles and view counts only).
   - Face: not needed in principle (the one I watched uses a face for the quiz format, which you would drop). Voiceover: yes. Dud: yes. Used by the 13: no.
   - Passes all four, but nobody has put it inside an opening. Low-medium certainty.
4. Wrong-place premise + physical sting
   - Videos: GERHZhnhRIU. Face: no. Voiceover: yes. Dud: yes. Used by the 13: partly (stunt premises at UnlistedLeaf, PokeNibbles, RealBreakingNate).

## (d) What Pokemon channels already do for humor (avoid)

From titles and top-8 videos by views (latest 40 Shorts each). I did not watch every one of these.

- ShortPocketMonster: "Should I Open it? Or Should I Keep it Sealed?" numbered episodes (1.4 to 1.9 million views each); a recurring format, not a joke.
- alexketchem: "What's Inside..." mystery-box titles with an emoji; curiosity, not comedy.
- DeepPocketMonster: charity pledges, "I Pulled HOW MANY Charizards", reactions to PokeRev. Hype and stunts.
- PokeRev: god-pack hype ("IMPOSSIBLE $5,000 God Pack FOUND"). Shock, not jokes.
- Leonhart: skits, POV, day-in-the-life, found footage ("A day in the life of a Pokemon master" 39 million, "POV: You're trying to find a good place to rip packs" 4.5 million). Lots of POV skit comedy.
- RealBreakingNate: lying-to-wife skits ("She's Mad, I Lied About Buying Pokemon Cards!" 2.5 million), 5-second challenges, kid reactions. Skit and prank.
- Purplecliffe: gamer shenanigans ("If you laugh, you lose your nintendo switch" 3.4 million, "Simon Says"). Face humor.
- UnlistedLeaf: stunts ("EATING ALL MCDONALDS X POKEMON CARDS" 2.5 million, "SHEET OF POKEMON CARDS GOES INTO A SLEEVE" 10 million).
- PokeDaxi: Pokemon GO, not cards.
- pokecate: family pack battles with caps-lock chaos ("CHAOS SWITCHAROO BATTLE", "MY SON HITS AN SIR").
- SimplyUnlucky: story titles ("I Thought Everything was Ruined And Then..."), Yugioh included. Cliffhanger, not joke.
- iPokeTCG: "GUARANTEED HOLO" and error-card hype.
- PokeNibbles: scalper and store humor with a one-line caption ("Took everything but the body spray. Scalpers stay musty", 6.8 million), trick-or-treat packs.

Net. Their humor is: face reactions, skits and POVs, pranks and stunts, price shock, caption one-liners. Their openings are not funny in the opening itself. That is the white space.

## (e) What I could not verify

- Reddit: three web searches found no useful "funniest opening channel" thread. Results were forum noise and unrelated pages. Leads that did appear (Leonhart for odd openings, "Divorced Dads" TikTok parody pack by Graeme Barrett at 5.8 million views, PackGod roasts) were not opened. TikTok pages were not fetched.
- Optional Rules: could not find who runs it or whether the voice is human or processed; sounds human. Could not confirm 62M and 44M are organic (no analytics). One channel carries engine 1; if it is a one-off, that weakens the verdict.
- Engines 2 and 3 rest on 1 video and on titles and view counts, respectively.
- Pokemon channels: judged from titles, view counts and prior notes, not full viewing.
- Several Pokemon-channel searches were useless: YouTube keyword search returned long videos and ads; hashtag pages mixed in non-collectible content (Roblox, kids skits). Searches for roast, rating, rage, deadpan and cursed with the packs returned nothing usable.
- Kinder egg, gachapon, sneaker and Labubu openings: scanned, none funny beyond reaction compilations. No sneaker opening with a joke engine found.
- First-laugh times are plus or minus 2 seconds; laugh beats are counts from short transcripts. Music-only videos have n/v.
- Whisper "base" mishears made-up names; the quoted fake card names come from its output and could be misspelled.
- Download count: 40 (the cap); 38 were from the first round, 2 later.
