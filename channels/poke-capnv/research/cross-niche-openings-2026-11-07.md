# Cross-niche opening research (2026-11-07)

Method. yt-dlp hashtag and channel Shorts tabs (YouTube search by keyword returned almost no Shorts, so hashtag pages were the sampler). 48 videos downloaded at <=480p. 37 are real collectible-opening or opening-adjacent content and were watched. 11 downloads were off-topic (listed at the end of table B). Each watched video: 9 frames (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3 s and last 6, 4, 2, 0.5 s) plus a 1 fps strip for the shortest ones, plus a faster-whisper "base" transcript.

Caveats, read first.
- Payoff times come from 9 sampled frames. Treat them as +/- 2 s.
- Whisper fails on music-heavy and non-English audio. "n/v" means not verified. Where speech is blank, the audio was music or noise.
- View counts and subs are as of 2026-11-07. Selection is biased: hashtag pages surface what already went big. Small-channel rows were picked by sub count (<50k) from the same pages.
- Genuine digital TCG Pocket openings, stamp unboxing and comic-pack openings were barely findable as Shorts. Rows for them are thin (see "Gaps").
- Hits per niche vary. Sports-card box-break Shorts from small channels did not surface; the sports rows are mostly pack/box rips from medium channels.

Legend. "Face" = a human face is visible at any point (PIP = small picture-in-picture). "$" = price or value shown on screen. Payoff = first moment the thing the viewer waits for is shown.

## (c) Structural rules from creators and analysts

Source quality is marked. S = stated by the creator or in a document believed to be his. A = analyst inference or secondary summary. None of these quotes was checked against a video or the original PDF: every one is relayed through a web page.

### MrBeast production memo (leaked Aug 2024; Rosanna Pansino posted it; authenticity reported as confirmed by multiple employees)
Source pages: [Alexander Jarvis reproduction](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/) (reproduces the memo text); [Zack Chewning excerpts](https://zackchewning.substack.com/p/content-lessons-from-the-1-youtubers) (cannot be verified against the PDF); [Tubefilter coverage](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=182149).

S (memo, as reproduced):
- Hook: "The first minute of each video is the most important minute of each video." / "This is why we freak out so much about the first minute and go so above and beyond to make it the best we freakin can."
- Retention: "The longer people watch, the better a video will do." / "For your videos to do well you must get their AVD and AVP as high as possible."
- Packaging: "The title and thumbnail on the videos you will be producing set the expectations for the viewer for your video."
- Pacing: "This includes lots of quick scene changes and highly stimulating simple content that reflects the story." / "No dull moments in videos."
- Re-hooking: "It's important to re-engage the viewer around this time because they could get bored of the story and click off." (Chewning's excerpt: "As soon as one storyline slows, introduce something new to keep the viewer guessing.")
- Stakes / escalation: "As you get deeper in the video the stakes get higher." / "...as long as things progressively get cooler."
- Ending: "The video endings must always be abrupt to protect retention." / "Strong payoffs at the end of videos boost retention." (Chewning: "If you've kept people this long, the ending has to hit.")
- Time compression: (Chewning, as memo excerpt) "Instead of spending the first three minutes on his first day, we'd show multiple days in that time."
- Memo is about long-form. It has no Shorts section in the excerpts found.

### MrBeast interview (Colin and Samir, 2023-06-27)
Source: [transcript summary page](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/a-brutally-honest-conversation-with-mrbeast). Auto-generated transcript; speaker labels unclear in places. Only the first 100,000 of 160,677 characters were read.
S: "I roast the hell out of people who like have boring first five seconds."
S: He has to "visually convince you to click on the video." Captions in the first five seconds: he agreed "100%".
S (on Shorts): the first frame of a Short is its thumbnail. He sometimes removes himself from the frame in vertical video to focus on another person's reaction, because the frame is hard to fill. His Shorts "lose so much money" but help reach.
S: Rule he dropped: he threw out "preconceived notions" about keeping challenges simple for one video. Surprise helps; repeating the same payoff desensitizes (this last point may be the hosts').
S: "how can I do that 100 times better" is his stance on copying a format.
A (not him): another summary page says he targets ~70% retention. Not checked. Do not rely on it.

### Jenny Hoyos (Shorts)
Source: [AI-generated summary of her interview](https://videohighlight.com/v/As7abwNhG7Y) and several marketing blogs. None are her verbatim words. A.
- A hook is critical; the first frame is critical and sets the tone. Visual hooks beat personal branding (recognizable locations).
- Foreshadowing: verbal hints of what is coming.
- Her scripts develop the hook and the final line first.
- Typical Short length about 34 s, based on her analysis of popular content.
- Story: "but" and "so" conflict structure; a story and a twist; personal stakes and irony; tasks in three steps.
- Readability at or below fifth-grade level.
- Loops: viewers looping to rewatch helps retention. The summary says retention matters less, then more; it does not reconcile this.
- Sharability matters, but she says she lacks data to prove it.
- One-second edits guided by retention data improved a video.
- Not found: text-overlay rules, ending technique beyond "final line planned first".

### Paddy Galloway (MrBeast consultant)
No primary text was readable. The Recapio page was an empty shell. One summary says: if the first ~30 s can stand alone as a Short, the intro is strong. All A, low confidence. Treat as unverified.

### Shorts distribution (analyst reading of a YouTube metric)
[Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/) reports YouTube Analytics now shows "viewed vs swiped away" and that thresholds are not published. "Test audience then wider audience" is a creator-site interpretation, not YouTube documentation. A. Fits the owner's own Studio data (hook decides the swipe).

### Why unboxing works at all
Only marketing blogs and parenting-app guides: anticipation, surprise, "slot machine" variable reward ([m2comms](https://m2comms.com/2023/07/24/unboxing-videos/), [screenwise](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/unboxing-videos-and-the-psychology-of-want)). A. No creator primary source and no study found. Treat as folklore.

Rules not backed by any source found: "3-second loops raise retention over 100%", "retention targets of 70-85%". These appear only on low-authority sites.

The last 3 s is one of: the value/total card (rows 1, 20), the reaction (rows 2, 3, 13, 16), the card held to the camera (rows 4, 8, 29), a series hook ("WATCH PART 11", "ON PACK TWO", rows 9, 34), a comment prompt (row 26 "What should I get next?"; row 29 asks a question). ## (e) What the evidence allows

1. Put the premise in the first second, as text and as the first words. Name the outcome or the stake, not "this" (rows 6, 7, 14, 21, 32; also the owner's #12 vs #8-#11). Every comparable flat row had no premise (12, 17, 22).
2. Give the viewer a ledger that a hands-only video can carry: cost shown early, value shown at each reveal, a total at the end (rows 1, 6, 14, 2, 3). It works with one item and no face (rows 1 and 6 reached 6.9M and 9.85M).
3. Keep it short and single-thread. Eleven of the 19 big rows are <=28 s; the owner's 62-64 s two-pack videos sit in the range where small channels flatlined (rows 12, 17, 22, 23, 31). Each beat after the first must be a new question (a pack counter, a chase, a bet), not another pack (rows 28, 34, 21).
4. Put the payoff either in the first ~5 s with a reaction/value tail, or in the last quarter with a promise held from 0 s. Avoid slow middle reveals (rows 6, 13, 14, 20 vs 12, 17, 22).
5. End abruptly on the value, the card or a series hook. Do not outro. One loop-like device (row 14 repeats the hook line) and one comment prompt (rows 26, 29) are the only structural re-engagement seen; neither is proven to lift views.
6. A bet or a choice can replace luck and costs nothing to film: a caption that sets two outcomes (row 4), a "will I get my money back" question (row 32), a guess-by-feel step (row 35), a day counter (row 34), a binary choice (row 9). All work with hands only and 1-3 items.
7. A face is not required for scale (rows 1, 4, 6, 28), but 13, 16, 26 and 18 show that where there is no ledger, a second person's reaction is what carries it. A hands-only format therefore needs a ledger or a bet to replace that reaction.
8. TikTok and the 1k YouTube ceiling are not explained by this sample. The only large-sample facts are that every one of the 7 sub-50k winners had one premise, one thread and <=37 s. Treat any claim that loops or re-hooks alone fix retention as unproven (section (c): no primary source).
